Typeahead expansion plan

Build larger n-gram and neural models on much broader mathematical corpora, then rank their completions using the tutor’s scoped preferences and the learner’s typed context.

This revision adds sixteen concrete improvements to the handoff branch, with proposed sizes, ranking behavior, an implementation order and measurable acceptance criteria. The earlier mathematical audit remains available below.

Promotion and demotion on one scale

s(c | h) = wᵀφ(c, h) + btutor(c, h)
btutor(c, h) = clip(β ∑matched entries priority × match strength, −B, B)

For the initial experiment, use priorities +1, 0 and −1, β = log 2 and B = log 4. A fully matched hint doubles or halves the unnormalized weight, with a bounded total effect. Match strength lies between 0 and 1 and is zero outside the hint's scope. Equal-specificity contradictory hints cancel. Tune these starting values on held-out ranking examples. The base features include language-model evidence, typed-prefix fit, touch likelihood, source-specific cache evidence, phrase position and local acceptance history.

Consider a learner asked to restate a definition, who has typed “the def”. The three completions below are assumed eligible. Their base weights are normalized over this illustrative three-item list only.

Use relations and phrase position

If the tutor gives “U is an open subset of Y” and “f maps X to Y”, the text “U is open in …” can rank Y above X while restating the given fact. A symbol table can retain the set's ambient space, the map's domain and codomain, and the scope of a local declaration. Missing or ambiguous annotations give neutral type evidence. A prompt asking the learner to choose a witness still requires the learner's choice.

For a wording phrase already in progress, boost its valid next extension and reduce other boilerplate openers that would interrupt it. Use the full phrase and sentence state, preserve punctuation, and stop its preference after completion. Apply a bounded bonus once to a whole displayed phrase. For token-by-token search, use a bounded prefix potential and its change at each step, so a phrase receives no repeated full bonus for every token. Duplicate or nested phrases share evidence.

Keep hard withholding and learner-choice eligibility outside the learned score. “Is f injective or surjective?” provides two alternatives. A tutor priority cannot itself establish which conclusion the learner reached. Check the exact insertion plus the surrounding draft before display and acceptance, including phrases that cross the caret. This permits strong contextual ranking among eligible candidates.

Sixteen improvements to the current branch

Where the scores stop being probabilities

The trigram’s counts are sound. Its packed scores drift.

The builder counts distinct left contexts for lower orders and estimates separate modified Kneser–Ney discounts at each order. The pre-quantization backoff weight redistributes the remaining mass over the omitted continuations. This is count-threshold pruning. The original design also discusses entropy pruning, which has a different selection criterion.

I decoded the 5,109,064-byte binary, verified its SHA-256 and summed all 30,005 possible next-token scores for each stored history using the branch's lookup rule. The total should be 1 for a probability distribution.

Normalize the cache and the neural blend

When the cache is populated, the branch discounts the base probability only for words found in the cache. For base probabilities (0.8, 0.2), a cache containing only the second word, and a cache weight of 0.1, the branch gives (0.8, 0.28). The intended mixture gives (0.72, 0.28). Apply the 0.9 factor to every base outcome. Use the full base distribution when the cache is empty.

s(w) = (1 − λ) log PKN(w | h) + λ log Pneural(w | h)
log Pmix(w | h) = s(w) − log ∑v∈V exp s(v)

For equally weighted inputs (0.9, 0.1) and (0.1, 0.9), exponentiating the mixed scores gives (0.3, 0.3). The normalized blend is (0.5, 0.5). Normalizing only over the shortlist gives probabilities conditional on that shortlist. Rank candidates with the score, then use a full-vocabulary distribution or an explicitly calibrated event probability when the magnitude matters.

Two proof examples behind the learner-choice rule

Preimages. For a continuous map f from X to Y and an open subset U of Y, f−1(U) is open in X by the topological definition of continuity. The claim in the plan is correct. Whether choosing that definition is part of the exercise depends on the checkpoint.

Compact subsets. In a Hausdorff space, fix y outside a compact set K. For each x in K, choose disjoint open neighborhoods of x and y. Finitely many of the first neighborhoods cover K. The intersection of their paired neighborhoods of y is open and misses K. Thus the complement is open. Offering “finite subcover” in this exercise would supply the central proof step.

Require zero observed displays on the blocked-case suite. For a separately sampled, representative set of independent opportunities, zero in 600 gives a 0.498% upper bound at one-sided 95% confidence. The two-sided 95% upper endpoint is 0.613%. Report the convention and sample unit. A curated regression suite establishes coverage of its examples.

Other mathematical assumptions in the original plan
  • Subject model and copy cache (§§5.4–5.5). The EM update is a valid mixture-weight update for fixed, normalized components on shared support. Smooth marginal ratios, give an empty cache zero weight, and normalize pointer attention over available positions. Shortlist-only rescaling conditions on candidate inclusion. The informative-prior log-odds z-score is an approximate distinctiveness statistic.
  • Phrase bias and grammar (§§5.6–5.7). A bonus of log 20 multiplies relative weight by at most 20 on that step. Treat it as a ranking feature. A grammar mask defines a conditional distribution only if legal mass is positive. Suppress offers on an empty mask. After \in, the learner chooses the operand. TeX also permits \frac{a}b, so a brace-only suggestion is an editor preference. Keep uncertain grammatical heuristics soft, including English “any sets”.
  • Distillation (§5.8). The temperature-squared KL loss is appropriate when teacher and student use the same outcomes. For different tokenizers, score complete word extensions with word boundaries. Record remaining probability mass or explicitly condition both distributions on the same shortlist. At the plan's assumed 15–30% of 65 TFLOP/s, 6 × 1016 FLOP takes about 51–103 minutes and 3 × 1017 takes about 4.3–8.5 T4-hours. The handoff's smaller budget puts teacher scoring in a later experiment.
  • Learning from selections (§§5.9–5.10). The pairwise logistic loss is valid for an observed comparison. Learn from candidates actually displayed together, record position, and keep unshown or stale items out of the negatives. Calibrate separately on later impressions. A 0.5 margin represents a 1.65-fold ratio on a natural-log probability scale. A learned score needs its own margin after the weights change. Start personal weight at zero without evidence and tune shrinkage toward the proposed 0.4 as local counts grow.
