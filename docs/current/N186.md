## N186 Atlas v3 joint ability reporting and project-aware resume queue

PR #1521 reports ability with a joint Gaussian approximation while retaining the existing prediction filter. This note describes the current implementation and supersedes the earlier cubic covariance relaxation, independently discounted historical likelihoods, and data-dependent batch-to-sequential switch on this branch. It does not claim an exact temporal posterior.

### Review corrections

The review of `6c8211c` found a discontinuity at the first temporal boundary. In production replay with a fresh `use` pass on November 1 and a hinted pass (`hint_level: 2`) on November 2, a first-attempt weight of zero gave variance 1.083860587453347. Weights 1e-10 and 1e-14 instead gave 1.068252050567747 and 1.068252050578482. A legacy partial attempt had the same defect: 1.457360269111604 at zero versus 1.495021465323185 at 1e-10. The difference did not vanish with the initial evidence. The old branch fitted the later hint/partial likelihood rows jointly only when no earlier covariance transition had occurred; otherwise it projected to a Gaussian after each row.

Every contiguous block of observations at one timestamp is now fitted **jointly against the incoming full Gaussian**, both at the start of a history and after transport. Timestamp boundaries apply the same approximation even when q=0 or the covariance is numerically unchanged. There is no weight cutoff and no `age.changed` decision about how later observations are fitted. This also prevents a negligible independent ageing component from changing a stable component's fitting policy across dates.

Instructions remain explicit Gaussian-projection boundaries in append order, preserving the existing `touch` contract. Production replay already orders instructions before observations at equal timestamps. The report does not claim invariance to inserting a new instruction boundary inside a simultaneous likelihood block, or to moving observations between timestamps: those change the approximation's event partition. Under a fixed instruction timeline, zero/negligible evidence and independent observations cannot select a different partition. An independent instruction leaves another component's existing mean and covariance unchanged immediately.

The previous follow-ups still lost process uncertainty when a new attempt arrived. In an actual replay with 60 hourly attempts, the reported KC variance after a five-day gap was 0.468173. An attempt of weight 1e-10 reduced it to 0.417844. At a longer gap the marginal had reached its prior, 0.504100, but that almost weightless attempt restored the stale variance, 0.415384. A constant likelihood also changed stable components when old KC evidence was reinterpreted as independent nuisance noise in each historical row.

The report now carries its **own full Gaussian mean and covariance** through time and instructions, then conditions new evidence on that aged Gaussian. It no longer uses the diagonal prediction filter's marginal variance to decide how much reporting evidence survives. Old observations are not refitted as if forgotten uncertainty had disappeared. A zero-weight, constant-likelihood, or cancelled predictor cannot advance its information state. Negligible observations converge to the same immediate and future reports as no information, including when an unrelated component is observed in between.

A separate numerical defect stopped the old fitter after twelve Fisher steps. For ten partial-retrieval passes with prior variance 1, guess floor .05 and amplitude .4, it returned mean 1.224447 with a nonzero score of .730852; the mode is 1.536720. The corrected fitter verifies stationarity and uses the **observed** likelihood Hessian for covariance. Fisher information is only a positive-definite search direction when needed, never a substitute reported covariance.

### The reporting approximation

The first observation block uses the original independent Gaussian priors. At each subsequent timestamp, the previous full Gaussian is transported and then conditioned jointly on that timestamp's likelihoods. This chronological Gaussian compression also applies to q=0 histories; it is not one global static MAP fit across different dates. Component vectors are canonicalized, and independent likelihood factors in the reduced Gaussian coordinates are fitted separately.

For a block whose predictors share one vector x (including ordinary hint ladders and legacy partials), the incoming covariance supplies V = x^T S x. The joint objective is one-dimensional, with **all distinct outcomes, offsets, amplitudes and weights retained**. Only identical likelihood sites can be reduced to one summed weight. For other blocks, B selects the union of active coordinates; K = B S B^T is positive definite even if the predictors are nearly collinear. With its Cholesky factor K = L L^T and W = S B^T L^-T, fitting the whitened coordinates z with prior N(0,I) gives:

`mean' = mean + W z_mode`

`S' = S + W (Cov(z) - I) W^T`.

This lift updates the entire covariance, including unobserved but correlated components. It introduces no rank threshold, ridge, covariance truncation or pseudoinverse. It avoids a dense solve over every registered component for each hint/partial block. Fitted latents are conditioned on; only genuinely omitted item/component uncertainty receives the existing probit attenuation.

Single observations and repeated identical sites retain the fast scalar solver. It brackets every stationary point by analytically partitioning the score into monotone intervals and compares their densities. This matters with a nonzero guess floor: a 29-pass fixture has two local maxima, and the earlier solver could select the inferior one. Its derivatives and log probabilities remain stable for saturated predictors and very small floors.

Mixed likelihood blocks use damped observed-Newton steps from the Gaussian prior, two continuously weight-averaged pooled starts, and two starts for each distinct likelihood family using that family's own weight. There is no top-k family cutoff. A vanishing new family contributes starts approaching the already-included Gaussian mean; the pooled starts retain combined support when identical observations split into slightly different parameter families. Predictor averaging commutes with Gaussian whitening. These are search initializations only: every candidate is optimized and compared on the actual joint objective, with the highest-density converged positive-curvature mode selected.

This includes mixed one-dimensional hint/partial blocks. It is a finite local-mode search, not a general global-MAP or universal mode-continuity guarantee. The reported covariance uses the observed Hessian; Fisher information is only a search direction. The inspection objective reconstructs the incoming Gaussian's center from the actual mode displacement, not by substituting a likelihood score that would conceal a nonstationary result.

### Time and instruction transitions

Let P_i be a component's prior variance, v_i its current fitted variance, q_i its specified ageing rate, and d the elapsed days. For the ordinary case v_i <= P_i, the marginal remains exactly the specified capped linear ageing:

`v_i' = min(P_i, v_i + q_i d)`.

A noncanonical likelihood can legitimately produce observed variance above its prior. Such an excess relaxes toward the prior at the same q/day rate, without clipping at zero elapsed time:

`v_i' = v_i + sign(P_i - v_i) min(abs(P_i - v_i), q_i d)`.

Time relaxes cross-covariances with `r_i = exp(-q_i d / (2 P_i))` and `S_ij' = r_i r_j S_ij`. Equivalently, the whole covariance is `D S D + diag(v_i' - r_i^2 v_i)`. The diagonal remainder is nonnegative, including the above-prior case, so the transport preserves positive semidefiniteness. Capped linear marginal restoration and exponential cross-covariance decay both compose across subdivisions of elapsed time. A positive-variance component can reach its marginal prior before all its correlations disappear; those correlations continuously vanish in the long-gap limit. Stable components with q=0 retain their learned covariance block.

This replaces the earlier prior-room cubic rule, which was not composable: inserting an almost weightless observation at day 3 changed a day-6 variance from 0.386194 to 0.319760. It also avoids discarding a nearly unidentifiable ancestor's useful correlations as soon as that ancestor's small marginal variance reduction reaches its cap. No display threshold or calibration assertion was relaxed to obtain this behavior.

Instructions are processed **at their actual timestamps**. An instruction adds its variance bump, capped at the prior, and retains the corresponding fraction of remaining marginal information: `r_i^2 = max(0, 1 - bump / (P_i - v_i))`. A full bump clears that component's correlations, including when time has already restored its marginal to prior. Other components' means and joint covariance remain intact. Elapsed time after the instruction is then transported normally. Both transition types preserve the fitted mean, as the existing reporting contract requires; this is a posterior-centered Gaussian forgetting approximation rather than mean reversion toward the original prior mean.

### Prediction isolation, capacity and duplicate features

The production ADF updates, served prediction probabilities, prediction trace, FSRS state, hypothesis weights, mixture weights and research path do not read this reporting fit. Regression tests compare all of those outputs with reporting disabled, including gaps, instructions and card reviews.

The existing cap remains 256 fitted components. Item difficulty and components beyond that cap retain the prediction filter's own means and variances. Repeated occurrences of one latent are combined before allocating a slot or calculating nuisance variance: coefficients 1 and -1 cancel, and coefficients 1 and -.4 contribute .6 squared times the variance. Neither recording nor querying mutates caller-owned parts. Identical hypotheses share the fitted result; diverged observations, instructions and timelines are fitted separately.

### Resume queue provenance

With multiple projects, a selected project's queue items come first untagged, followed by other projects tagged with their slug. Without a selected project, all rows are tagged. Single-project behavior is unchanged. Card facts carry their note's project into review actions as `home`; this supplies provenance without charging those reviews against a project's study-minute cap. Orphan review actions are explicitly tagged `[no project]` in multi-project queues. The prior review's queue corrections were independently rechecked and retained.

### Verification on this correction

The new production regressions reproduced all six reviewer cases before the fix: hinted and legacy partial attempts, each after weights 1e-6, 1e-10 and 1e-14. They compare mean, variance, probability bounds, prediction and retention with the true-zero-weight history immediately and at day 20, using a linear-in-weight bound plus numerical solver tolerance. Separate checks compare the complete prediction/ADF/FSRS state with reporting disabled.

In the final reproduction, the absolute variance gap between first-attempt weights zero and 1e-10 is 1.14e-11 for the hinted case and 2.68e-11 for the partial case, versus 0.01561 and 0.03766 before the correction. At weight 1e-14 the differences are within numerical optimization error. Predictions and the complete ADF/FSRS states match `6c8211c` byte for byte for all eight zero/tiny-weight fixtures.

Independent block oracles use a reconstructed transported Gaussian and direct Bernoulli log densities or canonical-logistic derivatives. They check modes, observed-Hessian covariance, scores and off-mode objective differences for shared, distinct, signed and nearly collinear predictors, including unobserved correlated components and reversed observation order. The static observed-versus-Fisher fixture now places its observations at one timestamp so its independent-prior reference remains mathematically valid; no assertion or tolerance was weakened. Further checks cover independent histories across dates and on either side of a fixed instruction timeline.

The independent audit also found that using the first likelihood as representative of a shared predictor could miss a better mode: one failure at offset -10 followed by twenty failures at offset 8 returned mean -0.096568 instead of -7.939351, while a 1e-12 predictor perturbation found the better mode. Its log density is higher by 6.19777. New regressions compare exact and nearly collinear predictors with an independent bounded scalar mode oracle. Another regression perturbs all 29 amplitudes of a repeated multimodal fixture separately, preserving the known mode even though every likelihood family becomes distinct. These tests cover the new family and pooled initialization rules.

Existing regressions still cover independently calculated scalar Gaussian updates, time-subdivision invariance, instruction chronology, above-prior curvature, component aggregation, peer-cache behavior, long-gap rebasing and frozen-input immutability. The existing ability-display, coverage, error and certification thresholds remain unchanged.
