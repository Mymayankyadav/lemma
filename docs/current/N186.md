## N186 Atlas v3 reports the ability from a joint fit

After the Atlas v3 activation (2026-11-19) every concept card showed "—" and no interval was drawn, although the predictions were sound. Two causes in `_shared/adaptive/measure.ts`, both in how the ability is reported (not how it predicts):

- **The uncertainty was overstated.** The filter keeps a variance per component (you, facet, ancestors, concept, concept×facet, learning slope…). One attempt moves all of them, so afterwards they are negatively correlated and the variance of their sum is smaller than the sum of their variances; the diagonal ignores that. On the production data the reported sd was 1.0–1.3, the exact posterior is 0.55–1.05, and the display rule (`sd > 0.8` is "none") hid every practised facet.
- **The mean was noisier than it needs to be.** The same approximation makes each update overshoot. In a simulation whose truth is drawn from the model's own prior, the filter's estimate is off by 0.9–1.1 (rms); a joint fit is off by 0.5–0.7. An exact variance around the filter's mean was not enough: its 80% interval covered only 62–75% (the old, over-wide one covered 90%).

`_shared/adaptive/posterior.ts` fits the ability jointly instead: for each hypothesis it stores, per attempt, the components touched and what the filter measured (amplitude, guess floor, outcome, weight, linear predictor), then finds the mode of likelihood × prior (Newton with Cholesky solves and a backtracking check) and the covariance there. `estimate()` reads its mean and variance; ageing (variance grows q per day up to the prior, and an instruction adds 0.5 to the concept) is applied at query time for what happened since a component was last observed. Predictions, the trace, FSRS memory, the hypothesis and PFA mixtures, the research harness and its gates are untouched: the prediction trace of the production data hashes identically before and after.

Measured:
- Production data (149 trials): practised "use" facets 0.56–0.86 sd (was 1.0–1.3); 6 of 15 facets now show an estimate with an interval, one is `shown`. Cards read e.g. `Use ~.68(.56–.81)s3`.
- Simulation, 80 learners × 3 concepts, truth drawn from the prior: the nominal 80% interval covers 88% (2 attempts a day for 5 days) and 83% (4 a day for 6 days), with mean sd 0.84 and 0.64; the old one covered 91% and 90% at sd 1.37 and 1.17, with twice the error. The intervals err on the wide side. `tests/unit/adaptive/atlas-uncertainty.test.ts` pins coverage (72–95%) and error (under 0.8 of the filter's) on 24 seeded learners.
- Certification was not what the old uncertainty blocked: the filter's inflated mean let clean learners certify anyway. With the joint fit the mean is lower and the sd lower, so certification is unchanged for strong clean evidence and slightly stricter at weak evidence (P 0.57 → 0.46 at one failure in four over 18 attempts), which is the honest direction.
- Cost: replay unchanged; the first estimate adds about 11 ms on the production data, 70 ms at 800 attempts, 160 ms at 3,000 attempts over 150 concepts (the fit is shared between the two hypotheses while their attempts are identical, which they are until a card review couples them).

Limits. At most 256 components are fitted (`Posterior.MAX`); item difficulties (one per question) and any component beyond the cap keep the filter's own mean and variance, entering as an offset and an extra variance, so a very large account degrades to the old behaviour for them. The facets with little data stay "—" by design (`understand` has 4 of 110 tags in production), and facets not practised for about two weeks drift back toward it as their uncertainty ages (spec 01 §1).

Open: the filter's overshoot also makes the served predictions too extreme (D2 slope 0.68 on production, recalibrated by the Platt layer). Predicting from the joint fit would address it at the source; that changes the validated predictions, so it needs the research gates re-run and is not part of this change.

## The resume queue names the project

A project resume listed the queue's items for all projects with no indication of which was which. With more than one project, the resumed project's own items now come first untagged and every other project's follow tagged `[slug]`; a resume with no project tags all of them; with one project nothing is tagged (04 §resume; the legacy queue named the project in each ref). The largest resume on the production data is 1,448 tokens against the 1,500 budget.

## Takeover audit (2026-11-19)

The review follow-up had restored each marginal variance but capped only the **sum**. As a result, at the time every component was back at its prior, negative cross-covariances could still make a multi-component ability look certain. The report now uses a coherent Gaussian ageing rule: each marginal grows at q/day or by its instruction bump up to its prior; the fitted cross-covariance is attenuated by the product of the components' remaining evidence fractions. Mathematically the covariance is D S D plus independent prior noise, which is positive semidefinite and becomes the independent priors exactly at the marginal cap times. Repeated appearances of fitted or omitted components are combined before calculating variance. The mean and prediction path are unchanged.

Regression tests now check intermediate covariance against this model, the exact prior restoration date, repeated features, and partial instruction bumps, alongside the existing observed-curvature finite-difference and queue provenance tests. The N186 block was removed from `NEXT.md` as required by repository item-PR rules. No production data was written; the previous production/simulation numbers above have not been independently remeasured after this patch. Independent review remains required for changes under `supabase/**`.

### Follow-up: old evidence cannot resurrect after forgetting

A further review found that variance ageing only at query time was insufficient: the next observation reset age/bump and re-optimised historical observations against the same time-invariant latent. Each observation now carries an epoch and accumulated evidence-survival factor for each component. After the diagonal filter update we retain its marginal variance. At the next observation, intervening q/day variance or an instruction bump determines how much old information survives; old predictor coefficients are projected onto the current latent, and forgotten information contributes nuisance uncertainty. Full restoration to the independent prior starts a new epoch, with zero survival of old component evidence. Other stable components may still learn from historical trials. The prediction/filter trace is unchanged.

This is **approximate** Gaussian process marginalisation, not an exact state-space smoother: forgotten process innovations are treated as independent across historical observation rows. In the full-forgetting case the previously over-tight component is correctly reset. Targeted regressions compare dense evidence followed by a long gap and one fresh attempt, and by an instruction bump and one fresh attempt, against a matching fresh baseline; partial forgetting is also tested. No new production calibration or runtime figures are claimed.
