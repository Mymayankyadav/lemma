## N248 The Pad's math keys as a latent scratch pad for one symbol at a time

Owner: "switching back and forth between keyboard in lemma pad can be little hectic … use keyboard as latent scratch pad for short symbol drawings like just one symbol." Behind `padLatentInk` (Build stage, default off). With `?flags=padLatentInk` in dev/test/e2e builds, the local Pad's math keyboard does the following.

**Tap or draw.** A touch that moves less than 10 px is a key tap, as before. One that moves further draws over the keys, starting on a key or not, and the key under it is not pressed. Two fingers scroll the keys. A pen stroke ignores a second touch.

**The strip.** A row above the keys shows:
- the three readings of the drawing (Detypify 0.3.0, the on-device model the tutor already ships), and Clear drawing;
- after an insert, "Inserted ∀. Replace with:" and the other two readings. One tap replaces the symbol in place, while nothing else has changed.

**When a symbol goes in by itself.** Probability and logic decide together (`latentInk/latentInkPolicy.ts`):

| Reading | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| confidence ≥ 0.83 and complete | goes in once the pen has been up 350 ms, so symbols can follow each other with no tap |
| confidence ≥ 0.83 but extendable (`<`, `=`, `∈`, `∖`, `−`, …) | waits; goes in on the next key, on a new symbol drawn elsewhere, or after 1.2 s without a stroke |
| 0.35 ≤ confidence < 0.83 | three plain choices; goes in only when the learner moves on without choosing, never by waiting |
| below 0.35, or mostly shapes outside the catalogue (digits, letters) | offered if anything; moving on leaves it out and the strip says "Drawing left out" |

- **Moving on.** A key tapped while the drawing is held puts the symbol in first, then the key. A key tapped while the drawing is still being read waits up to 800 ms for the reading, keeping that order.
- **Continuing a symbol.** A stroke continues the held drawing when it begins within 350 ms of the last lift. Later, it must start on or near a drawing that still looks unfinished: extendable, or unclear and lifted under 1.2 s ago. Learners write one symbol after another on the same small spot, so a finished-looking symbol is never continued. A first run with the real model showed this: a new symbol drawn on a just-inserted ∀ was merged into it.
- **Taking back.** A symbol that went in only because the learner paused on an extendable reading is taken back by a stroke on it within 2 s. Its drawing then continues, so a slow `<` still becomes `≤`.
- **Stroke limit.** More than 12 strokes is never read as one symbol.

### The cost model and the simulation (`latentInk/costModel.ts`)

For each symbol, the model compares two outcomes.
- **Choose:** scan the three choices (0.5 s) and tap one (0.45 s). If the symbol is not among them, Browse or redraw (4 s).
- **Auto:** a right reading costs a glance (0.25 s). A wrong one is noticed at once 85 % of the time and fixed from the strip (0.6 s to notice, then a choice). Otherwise it is found later (8 s).

The recogniser's accuracy comes from published Detexify-family results:
- Thoma 2015, Detexify and write-math data: top-1 error under 17.5 %, top-3 error 4 %;
- Kirsch 2010, Detexify: top-3 error under 10 %;
- Thoma 2017, HASYv2: top-1 accuracy about 81 %.

No calibrated ROC is published for Detexify or Detypify. The simulation therefore draws calibrated confidences from a Beta distribution with that mean and top-3 rate, then distorts them by an overconfidence temperature (1, 1.5 or 2.2) before thresholding. That gives the ROC (true- and false-positive rates of "auto-insert when confidence ≥ τ") and the seconds saved per symbol. Below is the ROC and the gain against always choosing, for the published profile at temperature 1 and concentration 2.5, with 20,000 symbols. Always choosing costs 1.09 s per symbol.

| τ | TPR | FPR | auto-inserted | errors among them | gain s/symbol |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.50 | 0.96 | 0.65 | 90 % | 12.6 % | 0.36 |
| 0.70 | 0.86 | 0.36 | 78 % | 8.1 % | 0.39 |
| 0.80 | 0.77 | 0.22 | 67 % | 5.6 % | 0.38 |
| 0.90 | 0.61 | 0.09 | 52 % | 2.9 % | 0.32 |
| 0.95 | 0.46 | 0.04 | 39 % | 1.6 % | 0.26 |

- **Choosing τ.** The shipped τ = 0.83 maximises the worst gain over eleven scenarios: three temperatures × three concentrations, plus HASYv2-like and weaker recognisers. The worst gain is +0.30 s per symbol; the best is +0.43 s. At τ, 58–85 % of symbols go in with no tap, and 3–13 % of those are wrong.
- **Break-even.** A perfectly calibrated reading is worth auto-inserting above 0.71.
- **When auto-insert loses.** If choosing were nearly free and fixing very dear, the search returns τ = 1, which turns auto-insert off. A unit test checks this, and that the shipped τ is still the simulation's choice.
- **The floor (0.35).** Leaving a symbol out costs noticing and redrawing it, which is more than a wrong insert costs. So a reading of 0.35 or more goes in when the learner moves on; below that it is most likely a shape outside the catalogue.
- **Online guard.** The device keeps a count of automatic inserts and of corrections (a Replace from the strip, or the keyboard's Undo straight after). The error estimate starts from a prior of one in twelve. Above 12 %, τ rises to 0.95. Above the break-even 29 %, symbols are only offered. The count is stored in `localStorage` under `lemma.pad.latentInk.v1`.

### The n × n grids (`latentInk/symbolGrid.ts`, `scripts/latent-ink-grids.ts`)

- **Rendering.** `npx tsx scripts/latent-ink-grids.ts` draws each of the 153 catalogue glyphs in the pre-installed Chromium with KaTeX's fonts. Each glyph sits in a 1.25 em box, centred on its ink and on the baseline. A cell is on when ink covers 6 % of it.
- **Choosing n.** n is the smallest size at which no two different-looking glyphs share a grid; pixel-identical glyphs may share one, and there are none. **n = 11.** At n = 10, ⊥/⟂ and +/÷ collide.
- **Extendable symbols.** A symbol is extendable when its grid is a strict subset of another's and holds at least 30 % of its cells; 44 are. The output is `latentInk/symbolGrids.json`, and a unit test re-derives its extension table from the stored grids.
- **Limits of the grid relation.** Font glyphs are not handwriting. On 19 known stroke prefixes, the grids alone catch 6: `−`→`+`, `−`→`=`, `∨`→`∀`, `∃`→`∄`, `∣`→`∤` and `→`→`↦`. They miss `<`→`≤`, `>`→`≥` and `=`→`≠`, because KaTeX draws `≤`'s `<` smaller and `≠`'s bars apart. Allowing shifts and scales caught more, but marked 148 of 153 symbols extendable, which would leave almost nothing to insert at once.
- **The extension relation the Pad uses.** It is the union of the grids, the catalogue's own extension families (`<`→`≤`, `∈`→`∉`, `⊂`→`⊆`) and twelve listed handwriting prefixes (`=`→`≠`, `∖`→`×`, `∫`→`∬`, `∼`→`≃`). A false "extendable" only delays an insert to the next key or stroke. A missed one can insert a symbol early; the 350 ms settle and the take-back cover a learner who is still drawing.

### Verified

- **Unit tests.** `tests/unit/pad/latent-ink-{model,policy,keyboard}.test.*`: 26 tests, all passing. They cover the cost model, the ROC, the threshold and floor, the grids, every row of the policy table, tap and draw, ordering, replace, take-back and the flag-off path.
- **Real model in Chromium.** The dev server ran with `padLatentInk` and the real Detypify model. A mouse drew a two-stroke ∀, which went in by itself after the pen settled. A `<` drawn next was unclear: it waited for a choice, then went in ahead of the next key, giving `+∀<−`.
- **Not tested.** No physical phone or pen; no held-out handwriting set to measure Detypify's real calibration on the Pad. The threshold therefore rests on the published accuracies and the robustness scenarios, and the online guard limits a learner for whom it is wrong.
