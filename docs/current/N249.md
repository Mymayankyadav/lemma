## N249 Draw one symbol over the tutor's keys

Owner: "remove the lemma pad shit whatever you made and shift the logic to the keyboard there. Don't change anything in the layout it should be same as before", then "Keep the flag on by default". N248 had put this on the Pad page's math keyboard. The Owner meant the tutor chat's keyboard.

**What changed**
- **Pad page.** It is back exactly as before N248: `PadMathKeyboard.tsx`, `LocalPadEditor.tsx` and `pad-math-keyboard.css` are byte-for-byte as at 260dae1. The `padLatentInk` flag is gone.
- **Shared logic.** The cost model, policy, grids and generated table moved unchanged to `src/features/tutor/input/latentInk/`. Their numbers and limits are in [N248](N248.md): τ = 0.83, floor 0.35, n = 11, the online guard. The generator is still `npx tsx scripts/latent-ink-grids.ts`, and it reproduces the same table.
- **Flag.** New flag `tutorKeyInk`, on by default at the Owner's request. A flag on by default needs a review under AGENTS.md. `?flags=-tutorKeyInk` turns it off in dev, test and e2e builds.

**The keyboard's layout is unchanged**
- No row, key, button, text or spacing is added.
- The ink is drawn on a canvas laid over the keys (`.tutor-key-ink`, absolutely positioned, ignores pointer events).
- What happened to a drawing is spoken through a visually hidden status.
- With the flag on and nothing drawn, the strip and keys render the same markup as with it off; a unit test checks this.
- At 390 × 844, screenshots of the idle keyboard with the flag on and off were pixel-identical.

**How it works** (`useKeyInk.ts`)
- **Tap or draw.** A touch that moves less than 10 px is a key tap, as before, with its preview, haptic and tap correction. One that moves further draws over the keys. The key under the finger ends its press through its own cancel path: no preview, no long-press digit, no character.
- **Delete.** It keeps its press-and-repeat. While a drawing is held, Delete clears the drawing instead of the answer. Straight after an automatic insert, Delete counts as a correction for the online guard.
- **Space.** It keeps its sideways caret slide. A stroke from Space draws when it first moves more up or down than across, or while a drawing is held or has just gone in. The bar of a `≤` often lands on the space bar.
- **Readings in the strip.** They take the strip's three slots with the symbol suggestions' own markup and styles, best in the middle (`KeyInkWords.tsx`). While the drawing is being read, the slots are empty. After an automatic insert, the middle slot is the inserted symbol (Keep) and the others replace it in place with one tap. Any other edit, such as a key, typing or a suggestion, ends the replace.
- **When a symbol goes in by itself.** The rules are N248's:
  - confident (≥ 0.83) and complete: once the pen has been up 350 ms;
  - confident but extendable (`<`, `=`, `∈`, …): on the next key, a new symbol elsewhere, or a 1.2 s pause;
  - unclear: only when the learner moves on without choosing;
  - below 0.35: left out, and the status says so.
- **Order.** Keyboard edits made while the drawing is still being read wait, in order, up to 800 ms for the reading, so the symbol goes in first.
- **Insertion.** A drawn symbol goes in as a strip symbol does: its glyph in prose, its LaTeX command inside `$…$`.
- **When it is off.** Drawing is off when the answer cannot be edited, during composition, and while the scratch pad, equation mode or Draw symbol has the keys' place.

**Review fixes (chat-775341#oyhi, on c1f6506)**
1. **(P1) Taps while a drawing is read.** Every keyboard edit made while a drawing is being read waits in order: taps, long-press digits, Space's slide and Delete. When the reading arrives, or after 800 ms, the drawing goes in or is left out, then every waiting edit runs. Drawing anything new runs them first. Before the fix, a second tap replaced the first.
2. **(P1) Selection changed while reading.** A drawing records the answer's edit target (the editor and selection revisions the input assistance already uses) when it begins. A change to the answer or its selection, start or end, drops the drawing, with a spoken note. Its symbol is also refused at insertion time, so it can never edit a different selection.
3. **Delete.** The click of the Delete press that cleared a drawing is consumed, after a tap or a long press.
4. **Long-press digit.** "Drawing first, then the keyboard's edit" now applies to the keyboard's edits themselves (`beforeKeyEdit`), not only to clicks. A long-press digit after a held `<` gives `<1`.
5. **Correction overlay.** While the drawing's readings, or its Keep and Replace choices, are in the strip, the writing aid's correction overlay is not shown and is not counted as shown. It returns when the learner moves on.

Each has a regression in `tests/unit/tutor/key-ink.test.tsx` that fails on c1f6506 and passes now.

**Second review (chat-775341#oyhi, on 27ed924)**
1. **(P1) Queued keys applied to the wrong place.** Queued keyboard edits are data (`KeyEdit`), not saved callbacks. They are applied only together with the drawing, in one guarded step (`onFlush`), through the composer's current render.
   - The step first checks that the answer and selection are those the drawing began on, and that the answer can still be edited. If not, neither the symbol nor the queued keys are applied anywhere, and the spoken status says what was left out ("1 key tapped meanwhile left out too").
   - A selection change, a change to the answer, read-only or composing therefore drops them explicitly instead of replaying them into the new state.
   - Before the keys give way to another surface (the pencil, √ or Hide keyboard), the composer settles what is pending while its target is still valid.
   - Delete on a held drawing still applies the keys queued before it, where the drawing began.
2. **(P2) Layout switch.** Switching the keys' layout (?123, ABC, a maths category) is not a change to the answer or its selection. A drawing's target compares only the editor and selection revisions, not the layout's `modeRevision`. A held `<`, then ?123, then `1` gives `<1`. A drawing still being read, then `a`, ?123 and `1`, gives `∀a1`.

**Third review (chat-775341#oyhi, on d7a3355)**
1. **(P1) Send.** Send, by button or Ctrl/⌘+Enter, settles a drawing and the keys tapped behind it first. It then sends the answer those leave, read from the history (`props.value` is still that render's) and checked again for sending: images, □ slots, read-only. Before the fix, the queued keys were left out of the message.
2. **Closing the keys.** Every intentional way of closing them goes through `closeKeys()`, which settles first: Hide keyboard, a touch outside the composer, Escape and the plain composer's Keys toggle. Opening the scratch pad, including from the unfinished-drawing icon, also settles first.
3. **Browse symbols.** It settles first, so a held `<` goes in before the browser opens and its timer cannot fire under it.
4. **A drawing not yet read is unfinished.** A nearby stroke continues it however long the model takes, so whether `<` and its bar make one `≤` no longer depends on inference latency.
5. **A cancelled stroke.** A cancelled first stroke leaves nothing held, so the next key goes in at once. A cancelled continuation leaves the strokes already drawn as they were: their idle insert is re-armed, or they are read again if they were taken back.
6. **One gesture at a time.** While a pen is down, or a stroke is under way, another pointer neither draws nor presses the key under it, and its click is consumed. Pen and touch can each be primary at once, so `isPrimary` alone is not enough. Overlapping finger taps while typing are unaffected.
7. **A model that fails at once.** Reading is shown before the strokes are submitted. A model that reports itself unavailable synchronously (a Worker that cannot be constructed) therefore leaves its geometry fallback showing, not empty Reading slots.

**Fourth review (chat-775341#oyhi, on 56e9b0d)**
1. **A cancelled pen releases the keys.** The keys' own cancel, sent to the key a stroke began on, is marked and ignored. Any real cancellation releases its pointer, before or after the drawing threshold and on the key or the keys' area, so a cancelled pen no longer blocks the next touch.
2. **Each refused contact is tracked on its own.** Each refused finger or palm is tracked by pointer id until its click is consumed. The click's pointer id is used where it has one; otherwise the click is matched to a refused pointer already lifted on the same key. The stroke's own click is consumed separately.
   - A pen's trailing click no longer clears a touch's suppression.
   - One refused touch no longer overwrites another.
   - The pen's own tap on a key a refused touch rests on still counts.
3. **Equation chip.** Opening an equation chip settles a pending drawing first. The chip's position is then rebased onto the settled answer (`rebaseSpan`; if the edit touched the chip, it does not open), so editing `$x$` after a held `<` gives `<a $y$ b`.
4. **Drawing chip.** Opening a drawing chip, or resuming an unfinished drawing, settles first and rebases the image occurrence. Updating the drawing then replaces that same occurrence; the stale-target check is unchanged.
5. **Unfinished drawing at Send.** Whether an unfinished drawing blocks Send is worked out from the settled answer: it blocks only while its image is still referenced.

**Fifth review (chat-775341#oyhi, on c57dcfd)**
1. **A refused contact's record lasts only as long as its gesture.**
   - An accepted pointerdown retires its own pointer id's record, since ids are reused and a mouse always has the same one, and every refused pointer already lifted.
   - Lifts and cancellations are seen anywhere on the page, so a release outside the keys counts.
   - A released record expires when the click is checked.
   - Disabling the keys or detaching them clears all records.
   - A keyboard or assistive activation (`detail` 0) is never taken for a refused pointer's click, or for a stroke's.
2. **Chips are followed through the actual edits.** When a drawing settles, each step records the range it replaced (`editBetween`). It takes the selection, widened only to the left, and checks that the text after the selection is unchanged. An opened chip is mapped through those ranges (`mapSpan`), not matched by text. A `[` queued before an image reference no longer stops that drawing opening. Of two identical `$x$`, a deleted one does not open and the survivor opens as itself.
3. **Delayed resume revalidates.** Unfinished drawing's 280 ms callback settles, then looks the image up in the answer. An image no longer referenced is not reopened, and the keys stay. `openDrawing` likewise refuses an existing image with no occurrence left.
