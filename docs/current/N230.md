## N230 Apple Pencil and erasing in both Tutor papers

The scratch pad and equation composer use one Pointer Events canvas, inline and in full screen.

- **iPad defaults to Apple Pencil-only ink.** Either paper's header settings can allow finger drawing instead, and the device/browser remembers the choice across both papers. Finger navigation remains available; touch/mouse cannot draw or erase even when they arrive before pen while Pencil-only is active. iPad detection also accounts for iPadOS desktop browsing, with the Mac platform plus multitouch heuristic. No native Android change.
- In finger-enabled mode, pen-down overrides an unfinished touch ink or eraser preview without altering committed strokes or Undo. Touch stays suppressed while pen is stationary and briefly after lift; released/reused IDs do not inherit rejected contact state. Existing touch strokes already committed or cancelled before first pen input are still kept in finger mode. Pencil-only mode avoids this ambiguity for ink by not accepting touch strokes at all.
- **Double-tap the Pencil tip on the paper** to arm the selected eraser for the next Pencil stroke. The taps do not write; the paper returns to drawing on lift or cancellation. This is tip contact on the drawing surface, not the hardware barrel gesture.
- A lone short dot is previewed immediately and preserved once no second tap matches, including when using paper actions or moving to/from full screen. Ordinary moving strokes draw on contact without waiting for gesture classification. The live ink path uses coalesced samples and is rebuilt at most once per animation frame; completed segments cache their path.
- **Eraser modes:** tap the icon for the current mode; long-press it, or use the adjacent accessible menu control, to choose Stroke-wise or Pixel-wise. The choice applies to regular and one-stroke Pencil erasing in both papers. In equation mode the eraser options and return-to-pen control sit in the header, not over the ink; widening the floating Read/Undo/Clear overlay had intercepted intended strokes at phone widths. Pixel-wise removes sections crossed by the sweep, including gaps between samples, leaving editable vector fragments. It commits a single Undo entry; cancellation reverts the pixel and one-stroke preview. If splitting a stroke would exceed the document's stroke budget, that partial step is skipped rather than dropping unrelated ink. Stroke-wise erasing keeps its existing whole-stroke behavior.

### Platform and hardware boundaries

Apple documents [Pointer Events and Pencil in Safari](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/safari-release-notes/safari-13-release-notes) and [desktop-class browsing on iPad](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2019/203/). Apple's [hardware double-tap event](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uipencilinteraction) is documented for native UIKit; this web implementation uses tip events and makes no claim about the barrel gesture.

Browser cases simulate iPad identity and pointer input in Chromium. They do not measure physical iPad latency or validate Safari's event ordering. Physical acceptance still needs both papers in iPad Safari/PWA, inline and full-screen (portrait/landscape): palm-before-pen and pen-before-palm; parked palms; Pencil-only off/on; single math dots and tip double taps; fast pixel sweeps; cancellation; Undo, Read, Insert and navigation. Also smoke-test Android finger drawing and rotation.

### Automated verification

- 373 Pad/Tutor unit tests (45 files), including pen-only input, navigation/hover, tap timing and geometry, the shared device preference, swept pixel clipping, capacity, cancellation and one-gesture Undo.
- 26 targeted Chromium tests across baseline ink drawing, full-screen/rotation, and Pencil integration. The new iPad simulations cover both papers, persistence after reload, immediate fullscreen with an ambiguous dot, and long press offering different visible eraser behavior. An Android-profile browser regression confirms finger ink still works on both papers. Against the prior build, both new iPad per-surface regressions incorrectly committed two touch paths; the follow-up prevents that.
- Production build and typecheck, PWA/asset checks, repository lint and whitespace check. Geometry's stored-stroke bounds are cached to skip non-intersections; this is not a real-device latency benchmark.
