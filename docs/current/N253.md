### Study design

The public development comparison uses three short and three long answers from each of Math StackExchange and MathOverflow. The 12 threads have 350 scheduled edits, including 221 boundaries and 129 started edits. The four-gram SHA-256 is `c97bacd0de80f08a66f65a76c610c8fa6076daaf70f328f4eb8be684e7ef9ba8`. Full-answer costs come from driving the editor with tracing disabled. Exact boundary hits come from separate captures of the visible menu. Each hit requires the intended insertion, replacement span, caret and selection. The strict trajectory counts one action per accepted edit. A separate trajectory accepts a suggestion ending in generated space and presses a real punctuation key, charging two actions when the resulting editor state matches.

The release gates are at least +2.0 percentage points of simulated keystroke savings and +1.0 point of exact boundary top-three coverage on a new natural holdout. Both paired 95% intervals must be above zero. The original count-model targets were 55% savings and 35% exact boundary coverage. The original neural targets were 60% savings, 45% boundary coverage and three points more savings than the selected count model. Grey text requires a whole-thread 95% lower bound of 70% literal exactness, 200 displays across 50 threads, at least 5% on-time eligible coverage and positive incremental keystroke savings. Human time and mathematical quality require a separate consented study.

### What the strip and inline provider do

The first menu challenger adds exact words and punctuation from the visible question, words independently typed in the current paragraph, scoped declarations and supplied phrases whose opening the learner typed. It preserves mathematical case, the replacement span and selection. The second challenger scores the eligible edits for likelihood of advancing the draft, including an outside option. Its loss uses the summed probability of all valid advancing actions. The fitted model uses 40 fitting threads with 1,263 observed slates. On the separate 12-thread pilot its loss fell from 1.7755 to 1.6699. A positive or negative Tutor priority is applied only to eligible, scoped alternatives. The experiment compares these priorities with neutral and reversed cases. The learned Tutor group weights remain zero until independently reviewed contrasts support an estimate.

The existing three slots still arbitrate between typing, notation and drawing. The inline provider runs on its own worker. It rejects worker replies and layout commits arriving more than 60 ms after the edit. It checks the document, caret, mode, Tutor context and proposal again at acceptance, and records provenance for one-step undo. In shadow mode it produces observations. In an explicit lab build it can also display a qualifying provisional candidate. The held production configuration continues to use the shipping setting.

At a 390 × 844 viewport, the pinned shipping and candidate builds both placed the keyboard at x = 0, y = 606.219, width = 390, height = 237.781 CSS pixels. Both offered “notable”, “notation”, and “notational” for the same typed prefix. The captured images differ in a small strip at the right edge. At 320 pixels wide, the inline lab and shadow controls also had matching keyboard and slot rectangles. ### Cleaning and corpus mix

The experiment preserved the display form of mathematics and kept a separate search representation. It preserved original character offsets across TeX, MathML alternative text, HTML entities and supplementary Unicode characters. Publisher and copied-passage links keep related documents on one side of a split. Only exact copies and closely matching revisions are removed within a register. Manual inspection found that a single publisher could also host unrelated pages.

The three training arms used 500,000 tokens each from a bounded 4,500-document training prefix, a common vocabulary, the shipping four-gram file format and a 20 MiB ceiling. The cleaned mix emphasized short public Q&A in the third arm. The 50,000-token diagnostic, with 45,213 scored targets, came from held groups in the training pool. The development retrieval test used a separate public slice and the same 259 lowercase word-plus-space opportunities for each asset.

### The optional neural model

We ran the existing 33.87M-parameter teacher on the very same eligible browser edits. It scored up to 32 candidates at each edit, using the visible question and the draft left of the replacement. Within a shared replacement span it ranked the literal edit, including spaces and punctuation, by its full sequence log probability. We checked the batched cached calculation against four separate full forward passes of lengths one through four. Their maximum absolute difference was 0.00000573 nats. The four-gram and teacher then used identical family deduplication. The gold references were joined after scoring.

The teacher scored 7,695 candidate paths at 270 opportunities. It brought 34 correct edits into the top three and displaced seven others. These are offline ranks before the UI preserves previous slots. They measure ranking potential. The previous FP16 browser run delivered zero timely word scores in 230 eligible opportunities. A student would need a physical-device timing result and a fresh end-to-end comparison to establish typing benefit. The timing probe executes one 192-unit GRU step, a 128-dimensional projection and 32 shortlist dot products in a Web Worker with fixed, untrained weights. The tied embedding has 16,384 rows. Its 2,307,328 parameters occupy 2,311,168 bytes with int8 weights and float32 biases. Under concurrent development workloads, Chromium 156 measured 1.4 ms p95 for worker computation and 7.1 ms p95 for the worker round trip across 256 warm requests. Starting the worker and constructing synthetic weights took 514 ms. No authorized physical Android phone was attached. The same probe can run inside a debuggable Lemma WebView with `node scripts/typeahead/expanded/student_probe.mjs --android --out phone.json`.
