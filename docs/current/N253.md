## N253 Mathematical typing and inline completion experiments

The control is **Settings → Tutor → Writing aid → Try faster typing** on shipping commit `421967853f6e070bced42751d9073a27dc3fd1cf`. It includes on-demand private model installation and the sparse strip at a new sentence. The experiment uses the existing three slots and opt-in. The strip, inline previews and optional neural model have independent release decisions.

### Study design

The public development comparison uses three short and three long answers from each of Math StackExchange and MathOverflow. The 12 threads have 350 scheduled edits, including 221 boundaries and 129 started edits. The four-gram SHA-256 is `c97bacd0de80f08a66f65a76c610c8fa6076daaf70f328f4eb8be684e7ef9ba8`. Full-answer costs come from driving the editor with tracing disabled. Exact boundary hits come from separate captures of the visible menu. Each hit requires the intended insertion, replacement span, caret and selection. The strict trajectory counts one action per accepted edit. A separate trajectory accepts a suggestion ending in generated space and presses a real punctuation key, charging two actions when the resulting editor state matches.

The release gates are at least +2.0 percentage points of simulated keystroke savings and +1.0 point of exact boundary top-three coverage on a new natural holdout. Both paired 95% intervals must be above zero. Grey text requires a whole-thread 95% lower bound of 70% literal exactness, 200 displays across 50 threads, at least 5% on-time eligible coverage and positive incremental keystroke savings. Human time and mathematical quality require a separate consented study.

### The optional neural model

We ran the existing 33.87M-parameter teacher on the very same eligible browser edits. It scored up to 32 candidates at each edit, using the visible question and the draft left of the replacement. Within a shared replacement span it ranked the literal edit, including spaces and punctuation, by its full sequence log probability. The four-gram and teacher then used identical family deduplication. The gold references were joined after scoring.

| Exact top-three edit | Four-gram shortlist | Teacher shortlist | Net difference | Paired 95% interval |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| All scheduled edits | 114 / 350 | 141 / 350 | +7.71 pp | +3.94 to +12.30 pp |
| Word and symbol boundaries | 43 / 221 | 63 / 221 | +9.05 pp | +4.55 to +14.15 pp |
| Started edits | 71 / 129 | 78 / 129 | +5.43 pp | +1.41 to +10.58 pp |

The teacher scored 7,695 candidate paths at 270 opportunities. It brought 34 correct edits into the top three and displaced seven others. These are offline ranks before the UI preserves previous slots. They measure ranking potential. The previous FP16 browser run delivered zero timely word scores in 230 eligible opportunities. A student would need a physical-device timing result and a fresh end-to-end comparison to establish typing benefit. The timing probe executes one 192-unit GRU step, a 128-dimensional projection and 32 shortlist dot products in a Web Worker with fixed, untrained weights. The tied embedding has 16,384 rows. Its 2,307,328 parameters occupy 2,311,168 bytes with int8 weights and float32 biases. Under concurrent development workloads, Chromium 156 measured 1.4 ms p95 for worker computation and 7.1 ms p95 for the worker round trip across 256 warm requests. Starting the worker and constructing synthetic weights took 514 ms. No authorized physical Android phone was attached. The same probe can run inside a debuggable Lemma WebView with `node scripts/typeahead/expanded/student_probe.mjs --android --out phone.json`. The phone timing prerequisite remains open and the student has not been trained.
