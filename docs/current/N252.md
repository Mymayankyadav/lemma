## N252 The tutor's keyboard follows iPadOS on an iPad

Owner, with screenshots of the tutor and of the native keyboard on their iPad: "This is how it looks on my ipad. And this how the native keyboard looks on iPad. I understand that keyboard layout should depend on the device. Keep the current layout for android devices."

**Before.** Every device drew the same Gboard phone layout (N220/N221). On an iPad its ten keys per row were stretched across the composer: caps about twice as wide as tall, 48 px rows, digit hints in the corners, Enter as a pill in the bottom row.

**Which layout.** `src/features/tutor/keyboardLayout.ts` decides:
- `ipad` on an iPad (an `iPad` user agent, or desktop-mode Safari: a `Mac` platform with more than one touch point, the same test the paper's Pencil-only setting uses) in a window at least 600 px wide.
- `gboard` everywhere else: the Android app, Android browsers, iPhone, desktops, and an iPad in Slide Over or a narrow Split View, where iPadOS itself switches to its phone keyboard.

`useKeyboardLayout()` follows the window width as it changes. The composer passes the layout to `TutorKeyboard`. On an iPad it also marks the keyboard `data-keyboard-layout="ipad"` and drops the strip's Hide keyboard button, because the keys have their own.

**The iPad layout** follows iPadOS's full-size keyboard. Every row is 12.6 letter keys across, so the letters are staggered as on the iPad:

| Row | Keys (widths in letter keys) |
|---|---|
| 1 | tab 1.3 · q–p · delete 1.3 |
| 2 | caps lock 1.75 · a–l · return 1.85 |
| 3 | shift 2.3 · z–m · `!,` · `?.` · shift 1.3 |
| 4 | maths ∀∃ 1.3 · .?123 1.6 · space 6.8 · .?123 1.6 · hide keyboard 1.3 |

- **Maths key.** It sits where iPadOS puts its emoji key. There is no dictation key, so the space bar takes that room.
- **Secondary characters.** They stand grey above their letters: 1–0, then `@ # $ & * ( ) ' "`, then `% - + = / ; :`, and `!` and `?` on the punctuation keys. Holding a key for 380 ms types its secondary character. iPadOS uses a downward flick, but a flick here would draw a symbol over the keys (N249).
- **No enlarged preview** above a pressed key, as on iPadOS.
- **Modifier keys.** They are in the tinted modifier shade, with lower-case names at the outer bottom corner. A lit shift turns light with selection-coloured text. Caps lock has a light that is lit while caps are locked. Shift still locks with a quick double tap.
- **Tab** does what Tab does on a hardware keyboard in the composer: it takes the shown continuation, or goes to the next □. Otherwise it types a tab character.
- **Return** types a new line, the same as Gboard's Enter. Its accessible name is "Return".
- **Number pages** keep Lemma's symbols in the same frame:
  - .?123: tab · 1–0 · delete; `+ − × ÷ = < > ( ) /` · return; #+= · `^ _ ' : ; ! ? , .` · #+=.
  - #+=: tab · `² ³ ⁿ ⁻¹ ₀ ₁ ₙ ° ′ ″` · delete; `[ ] { } | \ % # & @` · return; 123 · `· … ∘ ≈ ± ∞ ~ * $` · 123.
  - The bottom row shows ABC in place of .?123.
- **Maths category pages** are unchanged, with a hide key added beside Delete.
- **Size.** Rows are `clamp(44px, 7dvh, 62px)` high, which reaches iPadOS's pitch in portrait and gives about 53 px rows in landscape. The text sizes scale with the row height, so the grey characters stay clear of their letters in landscape. Short landscape windows (under 560 px high) keep the compact landscape rule.

**Word decoder.** The decoder's touch model (`ngramSpatial`) places its key centres in Gboard's frame, where A is half a key and Z a key and a half past Q. On the iPad they are 0.45 and 1 key past Q. An iPad tap is now measured from the first letter of its row and moved into Gboard's frame, so the decoder's spatial scores hold on either layout. Tap correction uses the keys' real boxes and needed no change.

**Unchanged.** Gboard's layout, geometry and behaviour on every other device (the Gboard layout specs pass as before), the strip, equation mode, Draw symbol and key ink.

**Tests.** `tests/unit/tutor/ipad-keyboard.test.tsx` covers:
- which devices get which layout;
- the iPad rows and their 12.6-key widths;
- the secondary characters;
- typing, holding for a secondary character, shift, caps lock, tab, return, delete and hide;
- the number pages;
- the decoder frame (it fails without the conversion);
- Gboard's rows unchanged.

`tests/e2e/v7-tutor-ipad-keyboard.spec.ts` emulates an iPad in portrait and landscape and checks:
- the keys, the stagger, near-square caps, and grey characters clear of their letters;
- typing `Hi, @XYz` with shift, hold and caps lock;
- the number pages;
- Tab to the next □;
- hiding the keyboard;
- that Android, iPhone and a narrow iPad window keep Gboard's layout.
