## N223 Equations inline, equation mode in the keys' place and one way to write by hand

The Owner asked that symbols and equations in an answer read as rendered maths, not LaTeX, and open for editing on a tap. Handwriting, symbol recognition and the scratch pad were to share one way in. After seeing a tab bar, the Owner asked to keep Gboard's strip as it was, with equation mode in Refresh's place, and for one row of tools on the scratch pad.

- **Inline equations.** In the answer, every `$…$` or `$$…$$` is an atomic chip that renders its TeX with KaTeX (`TutorAnswerEditor.tsx`). The draft is still stored as plain `$…$` text, so sending, copying and history are unchanged. An unfinished `$` stays text. The caret moves around a chip as one character.
  - **Tapping a chip** opens the Equation sheet: a live preview, the LaTeX, **Done**, **Write by hand** and **Remove**.
  - **Write by hand** opens equation mode to write it again; what is read there replaces that equation.
  - Closing the sheet puts the caret just after the equation.
- **The strip.** It keeps the layout the Owner liked:
  - Idle: ∀ ∈ ⇒, Draw symbol (✎) and Equation mode (√), spaced evenly and all the same size.
  - Typing: ✎ at the left, three word slots, √ at the right.
  - At a word boundary with next words: ∀ ∈ ⇒ keep their size, the words fill the middle, then ✎ and √.
  - The fix and continuation cards are unchanged: ✕, the word, and their own refresh.
  - **Refresh suggestions** moved from the strip to Answer actions (⋯). It applies once the menu closes, to the words the strip shows again.
- **Equation mode: the equation composer** (√ in the strip, or a tap on the composer's pencil, now named **Write by hand**). Only the keys' place changes, and it keeps their four rows; the band under them gives its room back (only the gesture inset stays), so Hide keyboard is a tap of Keys away. The strip keeps its frame: Keys at the left, √ pressed at the right. The Owner's layout frames the composer with its keys:
  - **Operations along the top:** + − × / = < > ( ) ^.
  - **Variables down the left:** x y n, and z k i t a b c f g as the column scrolls.
  - **Digits on the right:** 1–9, 0 and the decimal point, with Delete.
  - **Symbols along the bottom:** _ √ π ∞ ∑ ∫ ≤ ≥ ≠ and ↵ (insert the first reading).
  - **In the middle, the composition and the paper.** The learner writes as they would say it, `x1 + y1 = 1`. Delete takes back one written token (a sign with its spaces, or a character); with nothing composed it deletes in the answer. The paper is a sheet three times the window's width and 2.2 times its height. It scrolls both ways with two fingers, a wheel or the scroll bars (`InkCanvas` takes `panTouches`; the default stays three fingers). Its tools sit at the end of the composition line, so the whole paper is for writing: Search symbol, Undo stroke and Clear writing when there is ink, and **Hide equation keys**. That last one gives the composition and paper all four rows, for writing a whole equation by hand.
  - **Autoeq reads the composition** (`useAutoeqReadings.ts`), and the strip offers its readings rendered; a tap or ↵ inserts the chosen one as an equation chip.
    - N222's local rules answer at once. `x1 + y1 = 1` gives subscripts, powers and trailing coefficients, ordered by the words before the caret: "The circle" puts powers first, "sequence" subscripts, "linear equation" coefficients.
    - When the rules have nothing, its on-device autoeq model (4.2 MB, Lemma's own verified files, the shared ONNX runtime) is asked through the same worker the Pad uses. These are local files; nothing leaves the device.
    - The composition as written, as LaTeX (`compositionLatex`), is always last.
  - **What the paper reads joins the composition.** While there is ink, the strip offers what the drawing reads instead, and a tap adds it to the composition:
    - the whole equation, from Pix2Text MFR after a 650 ms pause. The first time, a **Read equation · 118 MB** chip asks before anything downloads;
    - single symbols, from Draw single symbols when that setting is on. The ink is scaled into its 320 × 116 paper and replayed, and it never inserts on its own here.
  - **Search symbol** (Owner: keep Detexify for symbol search, it is more accurate) moves the ink to **Draw symbol**, unchanged, with its own paper, choices and Browse symbols. The symbol chosen there comes back into the composition, and the composer returns; Draw symbol's keyboard button also returns to it.
- **Research behind the choices** (web search, 22 Nov):
  - For typed shorthand, autoeq (3.3M parameters, Apache-2.0) is the only small on-device text-to-LaTeX model found. MathSpeech's 120M LM, or a fine-tuned 0.5–1.5B general model on MathBridge pairs, would be hundreds of MB.
  - For handwriting, ink-on (CoMER, 7.2 MB INT8, onnxruntime-web) is a far smaller alternative to Pix2Text MFR's 118 MB, but its licence and maturity are unconfirmed. professor-euler's MathWriting model is CC BY-NC-SA (non-commercial).
  - MathLive's virtual keyboard (MIT) shows the same split of variables, digits and operators.
  - None of them is added here, because new dependencies need their own item.
- **Scratch pad.** The tab row and the wrapped Insert row are gone:
  - One row of tools in the strip's place: Keys at the left end, then Pen, Eraser, Hand, Undo stroke, Recognize equation, ⋯ and Insert drawing (now an ↑ action button). The row shares the width rather than wrapping.
  - The resize grip sits on the paper's top edge, and "Three fingers to scroll chat" sits inside the paper.
  - The canvas is at least 40 rem wide, so the Hand scrolls the paper both ways.
- **Draw symbol** is unchanged. In portrait it keeps the keys' height. On landscape phones its choices stand beside its paper.

The writing aid's fix and continuation cards in the strip (N221) are unchanged, with their own refresh; only the strip's Refresh suggestions moved, to Answer actions.
