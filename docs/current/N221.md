## N221 Owner phone review of N220 (22 Nov 2026)

### Answer keyboard: Gboard's geometry

The Owner compared the keyboard with Gboard on their phone: Gboard has more space below the keys, and its keys are taller than they are wide, where Lemma's were nearly square. The new sizes come from two sources. The first is the Owner's Gboard screenshot (1080 × 2396 px, which is 2.75 px per dp, or 393 × 873 CSS px). The second is AOSP LatinIME's published keyboard height of 1.285 in (205.6 dp, so 51.4 dp per row). Gboard's rows measure exactly 51.4 dp. Gboard does not publish its key sizes; the rest is measured.

| Measure (393 px wide screen) | Gboard | Lemma, Android app | Lemma, browser |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Row pitch | 51.4 | 51.4 | 44 |
| Letter cap, width × height | 33.5 × 41.1 | 33.8 × 41.0 | 33.8 × 35.1 |
| Q cap from the left edge | 4.7 | 4.7 | 4.7 |
| Keyboard top (strip) to first cap | 53.8 | 53.8 | 53.1 |
| Last cap to the screen's bottom | 58.9 | 58.9 | 10.2 + the gesture inset (at least 8) |
| Space bar x and width | 140.0, 150.5 | 140.8, 150.3 | the same |

- **Keys.** Ten key pitches across, with side margins of 0.56% of the width. Caps sit 1.3% of the width apart, a fifth of each row separates the rows, and corners are 5 px. A and L have a key and a half of touch area, with their caps half a key in. Shift and Delete are a key and a half. The bottom row is ?123 1.5, comma 1, maths 1, space 4, full stop 1 and Enter 1.5. Letters are 23 px, with the number hint 3 px inside the cap's corner.
- **Shades.** As in Gboard, Shift, Delete, ?123, comma, full stop and Enter share the tinted shade. Letters, the maths key (Gboard's emoji key) and the space bar use the key shade. ?123 and Enter are pills. Colours stay Lemma's theme roles.
- **Space below.** In the Android app, 5.7 px sits under the last row, then the 48 px band Gboard keeps for Android's keyboard navigation bar (or the gesture inset, when that is taller). Hide keyboard (⌄) sits in that band where Gboard draws its ⌄, 44 px from the left. The composer no longer adds the gesture inset a second time above the keyboard.
- **Browsers.** A browser has no keyboard navigation bar, and its own toolbar sits below the page. There the rows are 44 px (caps still taller than wide), the band is the gesture inset (at least 8 px), and Hide keyboard stays in the strip. This keeps the conversation's room the Tutor layout tests require (420 px at 390 × 844). The Android app is marked by `html[data-native-app]` (`src/platform/native.ts`). Landscape phones (under 560 px tall) keep the compact landscape rules.
- **Strip.** The strip is 48 px in the app and 44 px in a browser, and its gap below takes up the difference, so the first row lands where Gboard's does.
  - Idle, its icons are spaced evenly across it (∀ ∈ ⇒ ✎, plus ⌄ in a browser).
  - While a word is typed, Draw symbol at the left and Refresh at the right frame three equal word slots with thin dividers, like Gboard's grid and palette.
  - Words are one line in regular weight at 1.15 rem (Gboard's 13 px capitals). Words of 10, 12 and 14+ letters step down in size, and anything longer ends in an ellipsis.
- Gboard capitalises the first letter of a sentence by itself; this keyboard does not (answers often start with a variable). Gboard's left slot can also show the literal typed word; here all three slots are suggestions, because nothing is ever corrected automatically.

### Word suggestions: an n-gram and touch decoder

`src/features/tutor/writingAid/decoder/ngramSpatial.ts` scores each candidate word as log P(word | previous word, conversation) + Σ log P(tap | letter).

- **Unigrams: the complete lexicon** (`unigram-v2`, 114,780 words, 1.4 MB, `word<TAB>frequency[<TAB>logic]`).
  - N219's `unigram-v1` (49,232 words) added only 8,192 of the AOSP dictionary's words, so ordinary words such as *restate* were missing.
  - v2 keeps every v1 entry (frequency and logic tag) and adds every AOSP LatinIME en_US word that v1's rules accept: 65,548 more.
  - `scripts/writing-decoder/build-lexicon-v2.mjs [--check]` rebuilds it byte for byte from hash-pinned inputs: v1, and the AOSP source copied from the N219 branch.
  - `decoder/completeLexicon.ts` loads it with N219's size and SHA-256 check, and caches it. v1, `decoder/assets.ts` and `decoder/lexicon.ts` are byte-identical to the N219 branch.
- **Bigrams.** The existing mathlib4 bigram table and the context-word lists, Dirichlet-smoothed towards the unigram (μ = 2000), so `of t` ranks *the* first.
- **Cache.** The words of the tutor's question, the checkpoint title and step, and the learner's draft are mixed in at 10% (a cache model), so the question's terms rank higher. A name the question capitalises (*Cauchy*) keeps its capital.
- **Touch.** Each letter key records where it was tapped, in key units, in memory only. A tap's likelihood is a Gaussian around a key's centre (σ ≈ half a key). A beam over the letters near each tap finds the intended word: `hsve` → *have*, `wprld` → *world*. Only recorded taps can stand for a neighbouring key. A letter from a hardware keyboard, a paste or an edit has no position and counts as exactly the letter typed.
- **Slips.** Two swapped letters, one missing letter, or one extra letter that is not the first (`teh` → *the*, `functon` → *function*) cost a fixed penalty.
- **Real words are never corrected into other words.** A word the lexicon or the spelling list knows is only completed. When the typed letters begin real words, a correction must be likelier than every completion of them.
- **Guards.** The decoder only adds to and ranks the writing aid's existing word pool, so all of its guards still apply:
  - nothing beyond what strict or Off mode allows;
  - nothing in protected proof gaps (`take a in`, `then`, `by`, `=`);
  - no proof word from a short stem, and no next word the existing engine did not offer;
  - the two-accepted-words limit.
- **Families.** A decoder word yields its family's slot to the existing engine's word (*notation*, not *notate*), and can still fill a slot left over.
- **Proof-steering words** (because, by, as, since, so, hence, thus, therefore, then, consequently, implies, suppose, assume, clearly…) are never offered from a short stem or as the next word. The Owner found the aid pushing proofs along ("by", "because", "as"). They are offered only once 80% of the word is typed (*becaus* → *because*).
- Words apply only when tapped. When the lexicon finishes loading, an empty strip is worked out again; words already shown never change under the finger. `data-decoder="ready"` on the keyboard marks the point.
- This is the quick n-gram baseline (L1 in the N219 plan). Grey-text continuations still come only from the existing vetted phrase lanes. A small language model for them, the neural models and the phone trial remain later work. N218's branch has no language model to reuse: it holds a 20 KB learned ranker and probe reports.

### Symbol suggestions from LaTeX commands and short forms

`src/features/tutor/input/latexShortcuts.ts` matches the text before the caret against the symbol catalog's 153 symbols (TeX commands and aliases) by dictionary and fuzzy matching (the catalog's Damerau search). A suggestion inserts its LaTeX command inside `$…$` (`\alp` → `\alpha `) and its glyph in prose (α). Nothing changes until it is tapped, and Off mode turns it off.

- **`\name`**, a LaTeX command being typed: exact, then common prefixes (`\a` → α before ℵ), then near misses (`\aplha`, `\lamda` → λ). These take the three word slots ("Symbol suggestions").
- **ASCII short forms**, also in the word slots: `->` → `<-` `<->` `<=>` `|->` `=>` `<=` `>=` `!=` `/=` `~~` `~=` `==` `+-` `-+` `...` `^2` `^3` `^n`, plus doubled capitals for number sets (`RR` → ℝ, `NN`, `ZZ`, `QQ`, `CC`, `PP`). A letter pair counts only on its own (not inside ERR), and an operator only when not part of a longer one.
- **A bare name typed in full** (`lambda`, `forall`, `infty`, `Gamma`, `epsilon` → ε ϵ, `gamma` → γ Γ). Names like *mu* often start a word (*much*), so the symbol sits in the strip's left icon place and the word suggestions stay. Ordinary English command names (in, to, sum, cup, partial…) need the backslash. A near miss of a name (`lamda`) counts only when it cannot be the start of an English word.
- The existing engine's notation replacements (`x <=` → ≤, `eps` → ε) still apply, and when one is offered it takes the strip as below.

### The suggestion box is now part of the strip

The Owner found the floating card ("Spelling teh → the" over the answer) awkward. The writing aid's spelling or notation replacement, its continuation, and "no other suggestion" now take the strip, as Gboard shows a correction: the new word in bold in the middle, ✕ (keep what was typed) at the left, Refresh at the right. The answer still marks the typed word it would replace (struck through) or shows the continuation in grey.

- **When it shows:** for the word at the caret, for a continuation, or at a word boundary.
- **When it steps aside:** while a new word is typed with its own suggestions, a fix for an earlier word steps aside and returns at the next space.
- **When it leaves:** when tapped, dismissed with ✕, when the text no longer has it, or when editing ends the newly typed run.
- **Undo** after taking a suggestion is one key at the strip's left edge, so the symbols and words stay usable. The next keystroke retires it.
- A second simultaneous choice (rare) takes a second row above.

Tests follow the Owner's decision and keep their intent. Two assertions in `tests/e2e/tutor-writing-aid.spec.ts` required the old card to stay outside the keyboard. They now require it to sit in the keyboard's strip above the letter keys, still clear of the answer and the caret. The buttons, names and data attributes the other tests use are unchanged.

### Other N220 review fixes

- **Atlas.** The rail has a button for every subject, and the one in view is marked (`aria-current`). Only the rail's buttons take taps; the gaps between them pass to the map, so the larger rail covers less of the map than the old one.
- **Calendar.** The view picker in the toolbar is a plain bold label with a chevron, not a bordered pill. The overdue chip and Add all are both one touch target tall, with the same corner radius.
- **Progress.** The total uses the display size, with Your week on the same row. "Focused · range" and the comparison share one line beneath.
- **Session header.** Tutor's header matches the app's detail header: it runs edge to edge at 52 px with a hairline, and the title sits centred at body size.
