## N221 Owner phone review of N220 (22 Nov 2026)

### Answer keyboard: Gboard's geometry

The Owner compared the keyboard with Gboard on their phone: Gboard has more space below the keys, and its keys are taller than they are wide, where Lemma's were nearly square. The new sizes come from two sources. The first is the Owner's Gboard screenshot (1080 × 2396 px, which is 2.75 px per dp, or 393 × 873 CSS px). The second is AOSP LatinIME's published keyboard height of 1.285 in (205.6 dp, so 51.4 dp per row). Gboard's rows measure exactly 51.4 dp. Gboard does not publish its key sizes; the rest is measured.

| Measure (393 px wide screen) | Gboard | Lemma, Android app | Lemma, browser |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Row pitch | 51.4 | 51.4 | 44 |
| Letter cap, width × height | 33.5 × 41.1 | 33.8 × 41.0 | 33.8 × 35.1 |
| Q cap from the left edge | 4.7 | 4.7 | 4.7 |
| Keyboard top (strip) to first cap | 53.8 | 53.8 | 53.1 |
| Last cap to the screen's bottom | 58.9 | 58.9 | 10.2 + the gesture inset (at least 8) |
| Space bar x and width | 140.0, 150.5 | 140.8, 150.3 | the same |

- **Keys.** Ten key pitches across, with side margins of 0.56% of the width. Caps sit 1.3% of the width apart, a fifth of each row separates the rows, and corners are 5 px. A and L have a key and a half of touch area, with their caps half a key in. Shift and Delete are a key and a half. The bottom row is ?123 1.5, comma 1, maths 1, space 4, full stop 1 and Enter 1.5. Letters are 23 px, with the number hint 3 px inside the cap's corner.
- **Shades.** As in Gboard, Shift, Delete, ?123, comma, full stop and Enter share the tinted shade. Letters, the maths key (Gboard's emoji key) and the space bar use the key shade. ?123 and Enter are pills. Colours stay Lemma's theme roles.
- **Space below.** In the Android app, 5.7 px sits under the last row, then the 48 px band Gboard keeps for Android's keyboard navigation bar (or the gesture inset, when that is taller). Hide keyboard (⌄) sits in that band where Gboard draws its ⌄, 44 px from the left. The composer no longer adds the gesture inset a second time above the keyboard.
- **Browsers.** A browser has no keyboard navigation bar, and its own toolbar sits below the page. There the rows are 44 px (caps still taller than wide), the band is the gesture inset (at least 8 px), and Hide keyboard stays in the strip. This keeps the conversation's room the Tutor layout tests require (420 px at 390 × 844). The Android app is marked by `html[data-native-app]` (`src/platform/native.ts`). Landscape phones (under 560 px tall) keep the compact landscape rules.
- **Strip.** The strip is 48 px in the app and 44 px in a browser, and its gap below takes up the difference, so the first row lands where Gboard's does.
  - Idle, its icons are spaced evenly across it (∀ ∈ ⇒ ✎, plus ⌄ in a browser).
  - While a word is typed, Draw symbol at the left and Refresh at the right frame three equal word slots with thin dividers, like Gboard's grid and palette.
  - Words are one line in regular weight at 1.15 rem (Gboard's 13 px capitals). Words of 10, 12 and 14+ letters step down in size, and anything longer ends in an ellipsis.
- Gboard capitalises the first letter of a sentence by itself; this keyboard does not (answers often start with a variable). Gboard's left slot can also show the literal typed word; here all three slots are suggestions, because nothing is ever corrected automatically.

### Word suggestions: an n-gram and touch decoder

`src/features/tutor/writingAid/decoder/ngramSpatial.ts` scores each candidate word as log P(word | previous word, conversation) + Σ log P(tap | letter).

- **Unigrams.** N219's licensed lexicon (49,232 words: AOSP LatinIME frequencies, SCOWL spellings, mathlib4 ranks). Its files are copied byte for byte from the N219 branch: `public/models/writing-decoder/unigram-v1/**`, `decoder/assets.ts` (load, size and SHA-256 check, cache) and `decoder/lexicon.ts`. The lexicon loads when the keyboard opens.
- **Bigrams.** The existing mathlib4 bigram table and the context-word lists, Dirichlet-smoothed towards the unigram (μ = 2000), so `of t` ranks *the* first.
- **Cache.** The words of the tutor's question, the checkpoint title and step, and the learner's draft are mixed in at 10% (Kuhn & De Mori's cache model), so the question's terms rank higher. A name the question writes with a capital, such as *Cauchy*, is offered with it.
- **Touch.** Each letter key records where it was tapped, in key units, in memory only. A tap's likelihood is a Gaussian around a key's centre (σ ≈ half a key). A beam over the letters near each tap finds words whose letters were nearby, so `hsve` offers *have* and `wprld` offers *world*. When the typed letters already begin real words, a correction must be likelier than every completion of them. Typing on a hardware keyboard counts each letter as the centre of its key.
- **Guards.** The decoder only adds to and ranks the writing aid's existing word pool, so all of its guards still apply:
  - nothing in strict or Off mode beyond what they allow;
  - nothing in protected proof gaps such as `take a in`;
  - no proof word from a short stem, and no complete proof word replaced;
  - no next word that the existing engine did not offer;
  - the two-accepted-words limit.
- **Families.** A decoder word yields its family's slot to the existing engine's word (*notation*, not *notate*), and can still fill a slot left over.
- **Choice only.** Words apply only when tapped. Undo, refresh, feedback and personalization treat them like every other word.
- This is the quick n-gram baseline (L1 in the N219 plan). The neural models, learner data and phone trial in that plan remain later work.

### Other N220 review fixes

- **Atlas.** The rail has a button for every subject, and the one in view is marked (`aria-current`). Only the rail's buttons take taps; the gaps between them pass to the map, so the larger rail covers less of the map than the old one.
- **Calendar.** The view picker in the toolbar is a plain bold label with a chevron, not a bordered pill. The overdue chip and Add all are both one touch target tall, with the same corner radius.
- **Progress.** The total uses the display size, with Your week on the same row. "Focused · range" and the comparison share one line beneath.
- **Session header.** Tutor's header matches the app's detail header: it runs edge to edge at 52 px with a hairline, and the title sits centred at body size.
