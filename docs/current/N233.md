## N233 Next words that know the last words (flag `typeaheadV2`, default off)

The strip offered the right next word at 15 of 222 word boundaries (6.8%) on four sample answers: at a space only 14 filler words were
allowed, nothing was offered after let, if, then, by, = or ∈ until five letters were typed, 127 property and connective words waited for
80% of the word, and the "bigram" model had 750 pairs. With this flag the engine uses a Kneser-Ney trigram of maths prose.

- **Asset** `public/models/typeahead/ngram-v1/ngram.bin` (5,109,064 bytes, SHA-256 and counts in `manifest.json`): 30,005 words, 696,222
  trigrams, 296,649 bigrams, 8-bit log-probabilities and back-off weights; held-out perplexity 101. Built on one CPU core in about
  40 s: `python3 scripts/typeahead/build-ngram.py --input proofpile_dev.jsonl.gz` (the input's SHA-256 is recorded; `kn_build.py` documents
  the binary format). Loaded once, checked by size and SHA-256 and cached by hash (`decoder/kn.ts`); a failed load leaves the old strip.
- **Decoder** (`decoder/ngramSpatial.ts`): `languageLog` uses the n-gram when `typeaheadActive()` (flag on and loaded); the question
  and draft cache still mixes in at 10%. At a word boundary the candidates are the context's trigram and bigram continuations plus
  the commonest words, so the next word is offered. Words outside the n-gram's vocabulary keep the old frequency ordering below it.
- **Maths slot** (`engine.ts`): single letters standing alone in the question and the draft (f, X, U) are variables in play, the
  draft's latest first. At a boundary they share the three slots with the words in proportion to P(⟨m⟩ | last two tokens): after
  "Let" the strip shows the question's variables, not ∀ ∈ ⇒. No variable is offered after relations, ∈, take, choose, witness, by.
- **Learner-choice rule** (`learnerChoiceAllows`, replacing the blanket word list under the flag): a connective that steers a proof
  needs 5 letters and 80% of the word; a property or logic word (open, continuous, not, bounded…) is allowed when the question or
  the draft already used it, when the typed letters single it out among such words (`conti`), or once strongly typed, and never at a
  word boundary; every other word is allowed. At a boundary inside a protected gap (let, take, by, =…) no word is offered.
  On the pilot's sample answers the rule saved 57.8% of keystrokes against 52.0% for the old list (no guard: 64.5%).
- **Not done:** the n-gram runs on the main thread, not in a worker: a lookup is about 0.5 ms on a desktop, less than the cost of the
  message round trip. The next-word share on real learner text is unmeasured; the replay harness (a cleaned-up `pilot.py`) is still to be added.
