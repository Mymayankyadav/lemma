### 1a. LaTeX everywhere (#356)

Lemma is math-heavy; no text may show raw LaTeX.

- **One rule:** every string that comes from data or from the tutor is rendered through `MathText` (block) or a new inline `Tex` component (same renderer, inline mode, no paragraph margins). Covered:
  - project, checkpoint, stage and idea names; objectives, criteria and steps;
  - session goals, summaries, events and questions; misconceptions; diagnostics;
  - proposal reasons and plan errata; card fronts, backs and notes; comments;
  - source titles; search results; Atlas sheet blurbs; toasts that quote data.
- **Charts, timelines and the Atlas (SVG or canvas labels):**
  - Render labels as HTML overlays positioned over the drawing, so KaTeX works.
  - Where an overlay is impossible, use `plainMath(text)`. It converts common LaTeX to readable Unicode (\\mapsto → ↦, ^H → superscript, \\mathbb{R} → ℝ) and never shows a backslash or `$`.
- **Inputs:**
  - Every text field that accepts content (notes for the tutor, the card editor, the new-card sheet, project name, rename dialogs, Ask for changes) accepts LaTeX.
  - Each shows a live rendered preview under the field as soon as it contains `$`, `\\(` or `\\[`.
  - Errors show inline in the preview ("Unclosed $"); they never block saving.
- **Truncation:** clamping lines must not cut inside a formula. Clamp after rendering, with CSS line clamping.
- **Check:** `tests/e2e/latex.spec.ts` opens every screen in demo mode and fails if any visible text node matches `/\$[^$]+\$|\\[a-zA-Z]+/`. Demo data includes LaTeX in every kind of field above.
- **Sheet actions:** never show a disabled primary button as information ("Not in a checkpoint"). Show the fact as a caption, and offer only real actions.

### 1b. Graph paper background detail (simple)

| Screen | Curve |

| Today | $y = x^3 - 3x$ with its two turning points marked |

- **Night:** the same drawings on the dark grid.
- **Code:** functions live in `src/ui/backgrounds/graph/`, one per screen.

## 2a. Keeping subjects and projects findable (#352)

The owner will have many projects over time; the chip row and free-form subjects will not scale.

**Paths tab (the library):**
- **Search field** at the top. It matches project titles, subjects and source titles.
- **Status switch:** Active, Paused, Done. Archived projects sit behind an "Archived" link at the end of the list.
- **Grouping:** projects are grouped by subject. Each group has a header with the glyph, the subject name, a project count and a collapse toggle.
- **Pinned** projects form the first group (at most 4). Tapping and holding a project row pins, pauses, finishes or archives it.
- **Project rows:** title; a mini Metro (24 px) with the schedule chip ("2 days behind"); "now CP4 Compactness"; the percentage. Within a group, rows are sorted by latest activity.
- **Empty states:** "No projects match" with a Clear button; "Start one with +" when there are no projects.

**Project page:**
- No chip row. The title is a button that opens a **project switcher** sheet: search plus the 6 most recent projects.
- The checkpoint list hides the stage header when the plan has a single stage, and never shows "Unassigned checkpoints". Checkpoints without a stage join the nearest earlier stage, or form "Other" at the end.

**Today:** shows pinned projects first, then the most recently active, up to 4 in total, and an "All paths" link.

**Subjects** stay few and tidy:
- The connector's planning packet lists the existing subjects. `create_project` and `edit` match subjects case-insensitively and by close spelling ("Real analysis" → "Analysis"), and reuse an existing subject instead of creating a near-duplicate.
- Planning rule: "Reuse an existing subject when the field matches; create a new one only for a genuinely new field, and tell the user."
- Settings › Subjects: rename, change glyph, reorder and **merge** two subjects (moves projects and ideas; logged and undoable). Each subject is one Atlas cluster.

**Projects never seen by the owner:** self-test and system projects carry `projects.system = true`. They are excluded from every list, count, search, Atlas view and connector menu (#345).

## 3. Metro with schedule colours

Every checkpoint gets a planned date, so the line shows whether you are early, on time or late.

- **Data:** `checkpoints.due_on date null`.
  - The tutor sets it when planning (`propose_plan` and `propose_plan_change`: `checkpoints[].due_on`).
  - Where it is missing, `src/lib/schedule.ts` (and the connector) spreads the unfinished checkpoints evenly by step count between today and `projects.target_date`.
  - With no target date and no due dates, the line has no schedule colours.
- **Status of a station** (finished checkpoints use `demonstrated_at`; the current one uses the projected finish at the current pace):

| Status | Rule | Track colour leading into the station | Extra cue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| early | finished 3 or more days before `due_on` | `--good` | double rule |
| on time | within 2 days | `--accent` | solid |
| late | 3 or more days after `due_on` (or the current one projected to be) | `--bad` | dashed |
| ahead of plan, not due | future stations | `--ink-3` at 40 % | thin |

- **Card header:** a status chip: "On schedule", "3 days ahead" or "5 days behind" (the current checkpoint's difference).
- **Station sheet:** "Planned 12 Nov, done 9 Nov: 3 days early" or "Planned 12 Nov, expected 17 Nov at your pace".
- **Legend:** one small line under the project's Metro on the project page: early, on time, late, each with its colour and pattern.
- **Layout:** Metro keeps the lab layout. Stage segments are separated by small gaps; stage ends are larger stations; stage names alternate above and below the line and are shown only when they fit.
- **Connector:** `resume`'s `GOAL` line adds "| 5 days behind plan" or "| on schedule". The tutor rules add: "If the user is behind, suggest a smaller next step or a re-plan; never shame."

## 4. Atlas: a foldable outline of ideas with their links (owner, 30 Oct 22:38)

Supersedes the cosmos design. The solar-system words were an analogy for **nesting**, not a picture to draw: the Atlas exists to show **how ideas depend on each other**, and the hierarchy exists so that this stays readable as the number of ideas grows. Zooming orbits, minimaps and one-level labels hid exactly that, so they are gone.

**Shape.** One scrolling outline per subject: Field › Area › Topic › Idea › Detail (stored as `cluster`, `galaxy`, `system`, `planet`, `moon`; DB-4 and MCP-4 are unchanged). Rows are 44 px; each level indents 16 px with a faint guide line, like a proof's case structure.

- **Fold and unfold:** tapping a branch row (or its chevron) folds or unfolds it. "Fold to depth 1 · 2 · 3 · All" in the toolbar sets the whole outline at once. Folding is what keeps a large Atlas manageable.
- **Links in the margin:** prerequisites are drawn as nested arcs in an 84 px right-hand gutter, from the idea that is needed to the idea that needs it (arrow at the receiving row). Longer spans bow further out, so arcs nest instead of crossing. "Related" links are dotted and off by default (one toggle).
- **Folding never hides a link.** A link whose end is inside a folded branch is redrawn to that branch's row; links between the same two rows are bundled into one arc with a count; links wholly inside one folded branch are omitted. Folding therefore turns a web into a summary ("Topology needs 3 things from Sequences") rather than discarding it.
- **Study order:** within a branch, ideas are ordered by their prerequisite depth, then checkpoint number, then name. Prerequisite arcs therefore mostly run downwards and the outline reads in the order to learn it.
- **Tracing a chain:** tapping an idea selects it. Every idea it builds on (transitively) is marked blue (`--grade-good`) and everything it opens up is marked accent, both on the rows (a left bar) and on the arcs. All other arcs fade to 12 %. A small bar at the bottom says "builds on 4 · opens up 6" with **Details** and **Clear**.
- **Details sheet:** path (Analysis › Compactness and continuity › …), level and status, blurb, then **Builds on**, **Opens up**, **Related** and **Inside** as lists. Tapping any entry jumps to it in the outline: its branch unfolds, it scrolls into view and becomes the selection. Then come the checkpoint links, "Review n" and "Quiz me".
- **Status:** demonstrated is a filled accent disc; in progress is a half disc; re-check is a dashed warn ring; not started is a hollow ink ring. Branch rows show "done/total" and a warn dot when something inside needs a re-check.
- **Search and re-check:** "Find an idea" filters the outline to matches plus the path to them. Choosing a match jumps to it in context. A "Re-check n" chip does the same for ideas due for a re-check.
- **First open:** every field is unfolded one level, plus the path to anything in progress or due for re-check. The fold state and the Related toggle are remembered on the device.
- **No** camera, zoom, minimap, breadcrumb, orbit or force layout. Deep links `#/atlas?focus=<slug>` unfold to the idea and open its sheet.
- **Code:** `src/features/atlas/tree.ts` (pure: build, rows, arcs, focus) and `Page.tsx`. Unit tests are in `tests/unit/atlas/tree.test.ts`, end-to-end tests in `tests/e2e/atlas.spec.ts`.

### 4a. Plan proposal: checkpoint drawer

Reviewing a proposed plan must not mean scrolling a long list to find the checkpoint you tapped.

- The checkpoint map and the list both open a **drawer** (bottom sheet) for the tapped checkpoint. It shows:
  - stage, planned date, and "New in this change" or "Changed";
  - the objective, then "Done when" (the criterion);
  - the numbered steps and the reading (sources and pages);
  - **Builds on** and **Needed for** as checkpoint chips that switch the drawer;
  - ideas covered and the later re-check prompt.
- **Previous / Next** (and the arrow keys) step through the plan in order, with "3 of 12", so a whole plan can be read without leaving the drawer.
- The list below the map shows every checkpoint grouped by stage, one 48 px row each (code, title, planned date). New checkpoints have a dashed code chip. There is no "n more" truncation and no inline expansion.

## 5. Sources-first projects

The owner's flow:
1. In the app, create a project with just a **name** and as many PDFs as needed.
2. Keep **appending sources** as they come (class notes, problem sets).
3. In Claude or ChatGPT, say "Plan <name>" and explain the goals in words.
4. The tutor reads the sources, fills in the project details and proposes the plan.
5. When new sources arrive, the tutor proposes plan additions.

**App** (`UI-6d`):
- New project sheet: **Name** (required) and **Sources** (optional, multiple). Subject, goal, style and target are removed from the form. On the project page they show "Your tutor fills this in" until set.
- Upload accepts many files at once (multiple select and drag-and-drop) into a **queue**:
  - Each file has a row with its name, size, pages read and a status: queued, reading, uploading, saving, ready, or failed with Retry.
  - Files are ingested one at a time to keep memory low; the next starts automatically.
  - 50 MB per file. The same file twice (SHA-256 per project) shows "Already added".
- The project page **Sources** tab:
  - Lists sources grouped by kind, newest first, each with title, kind, pages, date added and a "new" mark until the tutor has used it (`plan_checked_at` null).
  - An always-visible "Add sources" button appends more at any time.
  - Each source can be renamed, re-kinded or deleted (logged and undoable).
  - Total storage used is shown against the 1 GB free plan.
- Today shows a "New sources" signal for each unplanned source: "Tell your tutor: update the plan for <name>".

**Data** (`DB-4`):
- `sources.kind text check in ('book','notes','slides','problems','syllabus','exam','other')`. The kind is guessed from the file name (notes, lecture, week, hw, sheet, syllabus, exam) and editable.
- `sources.sha256 text`, unique per project.
- `sources.position int` and `sources.added_at timestamptz default now()`.
- `sources.short` is generated unique per project from kind and title (`notes-w7`, `rudin`).
- New table `checkpoint_sources (checkpoint_id, source_id, pages text)` so a checkpoint cites several sources. Backfill it from `checkpoints.source_id` and `pages`, and keep those columns readable until the app stops using them.
- `projects.subject_id` becomes nullable (the tutor sets it later).

**Connector** (`MCP-4`):
- The planning packet lists every source (short name, title, kind, pages, added date, whether it is new).
- `get(what=sources)`.
- `read_pages` and `search` work across all sources, with an optional `source` filter; search results name their source.
- The planning rules become:
  1. Ask the user for their goal, deadline and level in at most three short questions.
  2. `edit` the project (goal, subject, target date and label, style).
  3. Read the outlines of all sources.
  4. `propose_plan` with stages, `due_on` dates, multi-source citations (`checkpoints[].sources: [{source, pages}]`) and the concept hierarchy.
- New-source rule: when `ADAPT` lists `NEW` sources, propose a `plan_change` that appends or updates checkpoints citing them. New stages are allowed, existing finished checkpoints stay, and the change sets `plan_checked_at` on approval.
- The self-test runs inside its own subject and is excluded from every owner total (#345).

**Plan history:** each approved plan or plan change becomes a numbered revision on the project page ("Plan v3, 12 Nov: added Week 7 notes, 4 checkpoints"), with the proposal's reason.
